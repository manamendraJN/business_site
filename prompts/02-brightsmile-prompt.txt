STORYLINE (this comes before design and before animation)
The visitor is someone who has put off the dentist because they are
nervous. The site must tell them one story as they scroll, and the story
must make them want to book a visit.

- What the customer wants or worries about: "Will it hurt? What will they
  do to me? Will they judge my teeth? How much will it cost just to ask?"
- What makes this business special: they go slowly, explain every step
  before they do it, and the patient can stop at any time with a raised
  hand.
- The feeling the customer should have at the end: calm and ready, as if
  booking is the easy part.
- The big idea: the page breathes with you and answers one worry at a time.

Build the story in these beats, one scroll scene each:
1. The hook: the worry, shown honestly. The page starts a little tense,
   with the worries floating in the air. The headline: "Nervous about the
   dentist? You're not alone."
2. The turn: the page slows down and starts to breathe, with a slow,
   steady rhythm the visitor can breathe along with. The headline: "Calm
   visits. Bright smiles."
3. The proof: each worry gets one short scene and one plain answer.
   "Will it hurt?" "What happens?" "What does it cost to ask?" Then team
   photo placeholders and sample patient notes labelled "Sample".
4. The offer: the treatments in plain words, each with what it is, how
   long it takes and a [price placeholder].
5. The ease: your first visit in four steps: say hello, we talk first, a
   gentle check, you decide what's next.
6. The invitation: the booking button appears right when the visitor
   feels ready: "Book a calm visit". Also offer WhatsApp for people who
   would rather ask first.

VISUAL CONCEPT
Frosted glass clearing: each answered worry clears one pane of misty glass
and reveals a bright, calm, sunlit room behind it.

SIGNATURE MOMENT
A slow breathing light at the centre of the page (in for 4 seconds, out
for 6) that the visitor can follow. As they scroll, each breath clears a
little more of the frosted glass.

DEPTH LAYERS FOR THE HERO
- Background: soft morning light through a large window
- Far: a calm, sunlit room with plants, out of focus
- Middle: panes of frosted glass, each holding one worry
- Near: the breathing light and the headline
- Foreground: a few soft, drifting light particles (subtle, not
  sparkles)
No cartoon teeth, no faces on objects, no toothbrush clip art.

KEEP THE HOME PAGE SHORT

  headline, one or two lines, and a link such as "See the full menu" or
  "All treatments" to an inner page that holds the details.
- A busy visitor should get the whole story in under a minute of scrolling.
- The pinned hero scene should take no more than about three screens of

RULES FOR STORY AND MOTION
- Every animation must express a story beat. If an effect does not help the
  visitor understand or feel the message, remove it.
- One main idea per scene, with one short headline a busy person can read
  in three seconds.
- The visitor must always know what to do next. Repeat the call to action
  after the proof and at the end.
- The story must still make sense with animation turned off.
- Text must appear in the right reading order at every scroll position,
  and scrolling back up must reverse every animation cleanly, with no
  missing, doubled or jumbled words.
- If a scene shows a "before" picture (a plain, dull or bad version), label
  it clearly as an example so nobody thinks it is this business's own page.

CREATIVE DIRECTION
1. Use the visual concept given above. If none is given, take ONE visual
   concept from the storyline and from this business's own world (its
   materials, products, place or story), and tell me the concept in one

2. Choose a colour palette and a font pairing that fit the concept and mood.

4. Illustration style: refined and believable, never cartoon. Use natural

5. If the business has no logo, design a simple, modern, geometric mark

IMPRESSIVE, NOT COMMON
- This must not look like a normal website. A visitor should feel within
  the first three seconds that they have not seen a site like this before.
- Do not use the common layout: navigation bar, hero photo with a button,
  three feature cards, testimonial slider, footer. If a section could be
  moved to any other business's site without changes, redesign it.
- Give the site one signature moment that people would describe to a
  friend, built from this business's own world and story.
- Design each section with its own composition: vary the scale, use bold
  typography, overlap elements, break the grid on purpose.
- Before you finish, check every section against this question: "Would
  this appear on a template site?" If yes, redo that section.
- Impressive must never mean confusing. The message, prices and the way to
  contact the business must stay easy to find, and the site must stay fast
  on a phone.

IMAGES AND 3D DEPTH
- Create original images that belong to this business's world: its
  materials, setting, tools, ingredients, atmosphere and landscape. Draw
  them with SVG, canvas or CSS, or generate them if you have an image tool.
- Build every key image as separate depth layers, not one flat picture:
  background, far, middle, near and foreground, each as its own element.
- Use those layers to create real 3D depth: place them at different
  distances, move them at different speeds on scroll and on mouse movement,
  let near layers pass the viewer, and add shadow, blur or dimming to far
  layers so the distance feels real.
- Elements should emerge from the background and travel toward the viewer
  as the story moves forward, and each such movement must carry a story beat.
- All images share one art style, palette and lighting so the site feels
  like one world.
- No seams: when layers move, no straight edges, bands, boxes or gaps may
  show. Objects in a scene must not touch or overlap awkwardly (for
  example, a tree must not touch a house).
- Shadows on shaped or torn edges must not spill into the next section, and
  two shaped edges must never stack on top of each other.
- Do NOT create or invent images of the business's actual products, real
  people, the shop or the team. For those, add a clearly labelled
  placeholder at the correct size and shape so I can drop the real photo
  in later.

MOTION TECHNIQUES AVAILABLE (use only the ones the story needs)
- Scroll-driven animation with scroll scrubbing and scroll pinning
- Parallax layers with 3D perspective / Z-axis zoom
- Smooth scrolling
- Mouse parallax
- Kinetic typography and scroll-triggered text reveal
- Seamless transitions between scenes: the next section rises over the
  previous one while it sinks back and darkens
- A horizontal scroll section
- Stacking cards with depth shading (the covered card darkens and shrinks
  as the next one rises over it). Full-width sections must sink back and
  darken instead of shrinking, because shrinking shows their side edges.
- Custom SVG shape dividers that match the concept (not a straight line)
- Micro-interactions: custom cursor, magnetic button, hover states
- A subtle grain or texture overlay
- Animated page transitions in the same style

- Frame every hero scene for a tall, narrow screen so the key subject stays

TECHNICAL RULES
- Use the latest stable Astro with TypeScript.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS. Put all colours, fonts and spacing in one place as
  design tokens, and use only those tokens.
- Motion: GSAP with ScrollTrigger for scroll scenes, Lenis for smooth
  scrolling. Run animation code on the client only, and clean it up and
  re-initialise it on page navigation (Astro ClientRouter: start on
  astro:page-load, clean up on astro:before-swap).
- In scrubbed timelines, use fromTo() with explicit start and end values so
  reversing is always correct.
- In-page links (#section) must land just below the fixed header, both on
  the same page and when arriving from another page. Make sure the page
  router and the smooth scroller don't fight over the scroll.
- Structure: one component per scene in /components, shared Header and
  Footer in the main layout, all business text and product data in one
  /content file so I can edit it without touching the design.
- Use the framework's built-in font and image optimisation. Keep source
  photos under about 2000px wide and 500 KB so the repository stays small.
- Give every page its own title and meta description, plus a favicon, an
  apple-touch-icon and a social preview image.
- The contact form must be ready to connect to Formspree.
- Hosting: Cloudflare Pages. Use static output unless
  a feature really needs a server.
- Fully responsive, and it must work well on phones.
- Animate only transform and opacity.
- Include a still version for visitors who use prefers-reduced-motion.
- All text must be readable before any scrolling happens.
- Keyboard and screen-reader friendly: visible focus, alt text, labelled
  form fields, Escape closes any open panel.
